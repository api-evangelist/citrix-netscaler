<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Quantum Threat is No Longer Theoretical</h2>



<p>Today, every sensitive piece of data you create, transmit, and store is encrypted. The algorithms that have underpinned modern encryption standards have generally been viewed as robust and “unbreakable” —- but that foundation is about to collapse.</p>



<p>Current encryption standards like RSA or ECDHE, which is infeasible to decrypt by brute force, can be cracked in a matter of hours with quantum computers.&nbsp;</p>



<p>But what’s the real danger? Attackers don’t need quantum computers to start exploiting this. <strong>They’re already collecting encrypted data as you read this right now, knowing they can decrypt it later once quantum computers mature.&nbsp;</strong></p>



<p>This is the essence of the <a href="https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/11/03/1039171/hackers-quantum-computers-us-homeland-security-cryptography/">“Harvest Now, Decrypt Later” (HNDL</a>) attack. HNDL represents a silent and irreversible threat to decades of sensitive communications and creates a fundamentally different threat model than traditional security breaches.</p>



<p>Consider the scope: every document, every encrypted database backup, every secure communication your organization has transmitted that has a lifespan of 5 or more years, is potentially sitting in a bad actor’s data warehouse, waiting for quantum decryption. When those quantum computers become available, decades of communications that were presumed secure become instantly readable.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Large-scale quantum computers that can break current encryption standards could become practical in the next 5 to 10 years. This time horizon means that this isn’t a problem for tomorrow’s CISOs but a strategic imperative for today’s business leaders. And the scariest part is that no-one will know—-there’s no telltale signs such as ransom demands. It’s a silent timebomb, just waiting for quantum to arrive.&nbsp;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Who is most at risk?&nbsp;</h2>



<p>The organizations most at risk are predominantly those that handle long-lived, sensitive data, especially governments, healthcare providers, financial institutions, and other regulated industries. Long term data that is relevant for 5 to 10 years is a prime target for a HNDL attack. This includes data such as:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Medical records and patient data</li>



<li>Financial statement and tax information</li>



<li>State secrets and classified communications</li>



<li>Intellectual property and valuable research</li>



<li>Even private conversations and encrypted backups</li>
</ul>



<p>All of it is vulnerable to decryption, unless companies do something about it, now.&nbsp;</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What should you do about it?&nbsp;</h2>



<p>The transition to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) isn&#8217;t a future problem but rather a current infrastructure planning requirement. Concrete vendor timelines and immediate testing opportunities are already taking shape, and the economics strongly favor early planning to avoid the cost and disruption of reactive migrations.</p>

<p>If that all sounds daunting, you’re not alone. <a href="https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/04/28/organizational-quantum-readiness-remains-low-poll-finds-only-5-of-organizations-have-a-quantum-computing-roadmap/">Only 5% of organizations</a> have a roadmap to address quantum computing. <ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Q2 2025:</strong> Begin internal validation of quantum-safe encryption in non-production environments</li>



<li><strong>Q3 2025:</strong> Identify and map all systems where data confidentiality is critical</li>



<li><strong>Q4 2025:</strong> Begin phased rollout, starting with external-facing services</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>NIST Requires Migration to PQC Standards—Fast</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>By <strong>2030</strong>, deprecated classical encryption (~112‑bit) must be phased out</li>



<li>By <strong>2035</strong>, such encryption must be fully disallowed, and PQC fully implemented</li>
</ul>



<p>Organizations that come out ahead will be those building modern, unified infrastructure capable of coordinated security updates across hybrid multicloud environments.&nbsp;</p>
